## What V6 controls

- the persistent physical prism/instrument and its real mass, depth, contact,
  camera language, and mechanical state changes;
- near-black composition, restrained typography, spatial rhythm, semantic
  illumination, and cinematic sequencing;
- distinct desktop and mobile framing;
- deterministic motion, reduced-motion behavior, static fallback, and
  renderer-loss recovery;
- visual quality of navigation, Proof Lab, onboarding, docs wayfinding, and
  evidence inspection;
- source preservation and parity between canonical 3D media and browser output.

The semantic color sequence is unresolved/risk red, qualified cyan, explicit
approval amber, verification white, and receipt gold. Color communicates state;
it is not ambient decoration.

## What V6 does not control

V6 does not authorize product claims, copy, product counts, availability,
pricing, providers, commands, routes, package status, payment status, launch
status, customer evidence, or roadmap completion. Its historical Stage 4
snapshot and reference copy are superseded by current repository contracts,
tests, release-candidate evidence, and `CURRENT-ENGINEERING-STATE.md`.

Reference images, generated media, and renders are never evidence that a
capability is implemented or publicly available.

## Runtime interpretation

- Product meaning, controls, quotes, approval, evidence, receipts, errors, and
  recovery remain semantic and DOM-owned.
- One deterministic lifecycle state drives both the semantic interface and the
  optional 3D renderer.
- Native scrolling and direct interaction may advance that state; scroll is
  never hijacked.
- The experience must remain complete under reduced motion, low power, slow
  network, missing WebGL, and renderer context loss.
- Mobile is composed deliberately rather than cropped from desktop.
- The finished result must be a functional product experience, not a static
  hero, decorative dashboard, generic AI template, or prerendered-video shell.

Current repository truth always wins if a V6 visual or copy reference conflicts
with implemented behavior.
