## C. Common Crawl decision

The unresolved issue is not access: Common Crawl permits free HTTP access. Its [Terms of Use](https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use) grant only a non-transferable, non-sublicensable license to the service, warn that each crawled item may carry separate publisher terms, require respect for copyright and other third-party rights, disclaim content lawfulness, and strongly recommend legal counsel before commercial use. Clervo has no per-document rights/terms classifier, takedown process, commercial-content opinion, or written legal approval proving that returning extracted archived content in a paid API is lawful across the corpus.

Recommendation: **keep Common Crawl development-only and replace it in the Stage 4 production pair** with the contracted Tavily fallback. Retain its bounded index-to-WARC fixture and local qualification proof, but do not return Common Crawl body content to paying users or count it as resale-qualified supply. The safest cleared alternatives are (1) contracted supplier output with explicit paid-API/redistribution rights, (2) first-party fetching limited to publisher pages whose current terms, robots/content-use policy, and licenses permit the exact use, and (3) curated public-domain or open-license corpora such as government/open-data and Wikimedia sources with required attribution. Using Common Crawl with restrictions should be reconsidered only after counsel approves a written use matrix covering snippets versus full text, jurisdiction, publisher terms, retention, attribution, opt-out/takedown, personal data, and copyright; absent that approval, replacement is the decision.
